# Initial GMU 54 desktop comparison

These saved **DEMO** outings use October 31, 2026, the conservative uncalibrated
Together profile, truck-access evidence, a 1.5 km viewing radius, no dark travel,
and a 25% movement reserve. Dates, limits and access assumptions are editable.
They are provisional opportunities requiring field/access review.

| Saved option | Full walking | Gain | Movement range | Off-trail walking | Matching terrain | Quality-adjusted equivalents |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Soap Creek AM or PM, one viewpoint | 0.78 mi | 116 ft | 0.73–0.98 h | 0.18 mi | 548 acres | 219 |
| Flattop Bench AM, one viewpoint | 1.32 mi | 163 ft | 1.04–1.41 h | 0.14 mi | 747 acres | 250 |
| Flattop Bench PM, two connected viewpoints | 1.53 mi | 170 ft | 1.18–1.60 h | 0.14 mi | 905 acres | 306 |

All walking figures include the complete return and repeated segments. Coverage
unions count each analytical cell once. Quality-adjusted area integrates existing
habitat preference and glassability; it is not literal acreage searched, deer
density, detection probability or guaranteed optical clearance.

Soap Creek is the easier of these saved morning options, with less walking and
climbing. Its off-trail component uses nearly the full conservative usable
off-trail-distance allowance, so a tighter off-trail limit can change that choice.
About 59 acres of its matching terrain have unsupported vegetation/context and
contribute no quality-adjusted value.

Flattop Bench offers different country and greater modeled coverage at more effort.
Its recommended AM position is only about 84 m from mapped date/mode-supported
motor access. Another plausible entry has a connected walking approach of about
2.2 km / 1.35 conservative hours to that position, sharing about 954 m of the selected
approach. This is strong competing-access evidence, not a quiet-country claim.
Soap Creek's selected viewpoint is about 579 m from mapped supported motor access;
the next known entry is about 1.4 km away straight-line, with no supported connected
walking result in the evaluated graph. That missing connection does not establish
remoteness.

AM and PM fit independently. Their combined-day variants fail the default shared
day: cumulative movement/off-trail limits and approximately 11.5 elapsed hours
exceed the limits. Rest does not reset effort. A separate integration case verifies
combined-day save/export when the owner explicitly enters sufficient limits.

The PM Flattop two-stop choice preserves roughly 60 minutes per stop and a complete
conservative return. Its modeled return is close to civil dusk; prefer the simpler
one-stop alternative or earlier window if conditions warrant more clock margin.
Civil twilight and observation windows are logistics assumptions, not legal
shooting hours.
